About this project

Business problem

Students and working professionals under sustained stress often don't self-report symptoms until performance, attendance, or wellbeing has already been affected. Waiting for a self-report or an infrequent check-in means intervention happens later than it should.

Business objective

Turn a short, thirteen-question survey into a binary risk signal — Low Risk or High Risk — that an individual or a wellbeing team can act on early, without requiring a clinical assessment to get a first read.

Machine learning workflow

  1. Data cleaning — duplicate removal, median imputation for missing survey responses, category normalization.
  2. Target construction — clinician-rated severity collapsed into a Low Risk / High Risk binary label.
  3. Feature engineering — a Wellbeing Index (sleep, activity, mood, screen time) and a Pressure Index (workload weighted by work-life balance).
  4. Encoding — label encoding for gender, one-hot encoding for occupation.
  5. Feature selection — features with negligible correlation to the target (|r| < 0.03) were dropped.
  6. Scaling — numeric features standardized with a scaler fit on the training split only.
  7. Class balancing — a nearest-neighbor interpolation (SMOTE-style) oversampled the minority class in the training set only.
  8. Model comparison & tuning — seven algorithms benchmarked; the strongest performer tuned with grid search over cross-validation.

Future improvements

  • Collect longitudinal data to track risk trends per user over time, not just single-point-in-time snapshots.
  • Add model explainability (e.g. SHAP values) directly on the result page so each prediction shows which inputs drove it.
  • Support authenticated accounts so individuals can privately track their own history rather than a shared CSV log.
  • Periodic retraining as more labeled data becomes available, with a versioned model registry.
